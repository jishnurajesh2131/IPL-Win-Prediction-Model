## 1. Original Problem

During the Phase 5 Final Pre-Training Audit, the feature `pre_match_prob_team_a` was identified as the **single critical blocking temporal leakage flaw** in `data/processed/live_dataset.csv`.

In the original pipeline, `pre_match_prob_team_a` served as a macro match-state anchor representing the baseline win probability of Team A before the first delivery. However, the mechanism used to populate this column across historical matches violated temporal causality: it applied the final Phase 3 Random Forest model (trained on the entire 2008–2021 dataset) globally across all historical matches in `live_dataset.csv`.

---

## 2. Why the Original Feature Was Leaked

The original feature generation suffered from two severe categories of data leakage:

1. **In-Sample Label Contamination:**
   For all 853 training matches in the 2008–2021 historical window, the pre-match probability was generated by evaluating the exact same matches that the Random Forest had already seen and trained on during Phase 3. The tree structure had already memorized combinations of `team_a`, `team_b`, `venue`, and form features for those matches along with their final outcomes.
2. **Future Match / Season Contamination:**
   A match played in season 2008 received a pre-match probability from a model trained on matches from 2009, 2015, and 2021. For example, team win rates, venue coefficients, and decision trees fitted on future cricket dynamics contaminated the probability anchor of the inaugural IPL season.

**Downstream Impact:** In the training split, `pre_match_prob_team_a` had artificially inflated predictive accuracy. Any downstream live model trained on these states would learn to assign excessive weight to this anchor, resulting in model over-reliance, severe test-set degradation, and calibration failure on genuine live matches.

Per the project's conservative operational rule, the feature could not remain in its original state. Rather than dropping the feature and losing pre-match situational context, **Option 1: Chronological Walk-Forward Pre-Match Anchor** was selected to regenerate the feature in a strictly leakage-safe manner.

---

## 3. Walk-Forward Methodology

A dedicated, modular generator was implemented in `src/live_prediction/walk_forward_pre_match.py`.

The walk-forward generator simulates an expanding historical prediction system:
> **"What pre-match probability would our system have produced at that exact point in IPL history if it had only known the past?"**

### Procedural Pipeline:
For every match $M_i \in [0, N-1]$ across all 1,234 eligible matches in `data/processed/pre_match_dataset.csv`:
1. **Identify Eligible History:** Select all completed matches strictly before $M_i$ ($k < i$).
2. **Extract Features & Target:** Build $X_{\text{train}, <i}$ and $y_{\text{train}, <i}$ using the frozen Phase 2 pre-match feature definitions (21 continuous, 3 binary, 4 categorical columns) and the binary target `target_team_a_win`.
3. **Fit Learned Transformations:** Fit `ColumnTransformer` (with `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore', sparse_output=False)`) **ONLY** on $X_{\text{train}, <i}$.
4. **Fit Random Forest:** Fit `RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=100, max_depth=6, min_samples_leaf=5, random_state=42, n_jobs=1)` **ONLY** on the transformed historical training subset.
5. **Out-of-Sample Inference:** Transform match $M_i$'s pre-match feature vector using the historical preprocessor, generate $P(\text{Team A Win} \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ via `predict_proba()`, and round to 4 decimal places.
6. **State Expansion:** Only after $M_i$'s probability is recorded does $M_i$ become available as a training sample for subsequent matches ($j > i$).

**Frozen Phase 3 Artifacts:** The Phase 3 model artifacts (`models/preprocessor.joblib` and `models/random_forest.joblib`) remain completely untouched and frozen. The walk-forward generator is an independent historical simulation whose sole artifact is `data/processed/walk_forward_pre_match_probs.csv`.

---

## 4. Chronological Boundary Rules

All matches are sorted strictly chronologically by:
$$\text{date ASC, then match\_id ASC}$$

For every match $M_i$:
- The training subset consists strictly of matches $M_k$ satisfying:
  $$(\text{date}_k, \text{match\_id}_k) < (\text{date}_i, \text{match\_id}_i)$$
- Match $M_i$ itself is **strictly excluded** from its own training set.
- All matches taking place after $M_i$ are **strictly excluded**.
- Player overlap on same dates was audited across IPL history and confirmed to be **zero** (no player plays in two matches on the same day). Therefore, `(date, match_id)` ordering guarantees total temporal isolation.

---

## 5. Cold-Start Strategy

During the inaugural 2008 season, the earliest matches have insufficient historical samples to train a statistically meaningful Random Forest.
- **Cold-Start Threshold:** The first 40 matches of the 2008 season ($i \in [0, 39]$) are formally designated as cold-start matches.
- **Assigned Prior:** Exactly `0.5000` (`cold_start_flag = 1`).
- **Zero Future Data:** No full-season, future, or synthetic information is used to construct the cold-start prior.
- **Transition Point:** Match index 40 (played on 2008/05/18, Match ID 336024) is the first match predicted by a trained model (trained on matches 0 to 39).
- **Audit Count:** Exactly 40 matches use the cold-start prior. From match index 40 through 1,233 ($N=1,194$ matches), all probabilities are generated via expanding walk-forward models.

---

## 6. Preprocessing Leakage Prevention

A common failure mode in walk-forward pipelines is fitting encoders or scalers on the entire dataset beforehand.

In this implementation:
$$\text{preprocessor.fit}(X_{\text{historical}, <i}) \longrightarrow \text{model.fit}(X_{\text{trans}, <i}, y_{<i}) \longrightarrow \text{predict}(X_{i})$$
- `OneHotEncoder` is instantiated freshly and fitted **strictly on the historical training subset** $X_{\text{train}, <i}$.
- Unseen categories (e.g., a newly introduced franchise or a new stadium in match $i$) are handled gracefully via `handle_unknown='ignore'` without throwing errors or referencing future category vocabularies.
- Zero learned transformations cross the temporal boundary.

## 8. Future-Match Perturbation Test

- **Test Name:** `test_walk_forward_future_match_perturbation` in `tests/test_live_pipeline.py`.
- **Methodology:**
  1. Select historical match $M$ at index 45 (past cold start).
  2. Compute baseline probability $P_{\text{orig}}$ via `predict_single_match_walk_forward(df, 45)`.
  3. Create deep-copy `df_pert` and aggressively modify future data ($k \ge 50$): invert match outcomes (`target_team_a_win = 1 - target`), and corrupt rolling form metrics (`team_a_form_last_5 = 99.0`).
  4. Recompute probability $P_{\text{pert}}$ for match $M$.
  5. Assert $P_{\text{orig}} == P_{\text{pert}}$.
- **Result:** **PASSED.** $P_{\text{orig}} = P_{\text{pert}}$ (delta = 0.0000). Modifying future matches has zero effect on historical predictions.

## 14. Matchup Shrinkage Reconciliation

### 1. Implementation Inspection
`src/live_prediction/matchup_features.py` implements continuous **Empirical Bayesian Shrinkage** with prior weight $m = 24.0$ balls (equivalent to 4 completed overs):
$$\text{matchup\_strike\_rate\_shrunk} = \frac{\text{prior\_runs} + m \times \left(\frac{\text{historical\_batter\_strike\_rate}}{100}\right)}{\text{prior\_balls} + m} \times 100, \quad m = 24.0 \text{ balls}$$

When $\text{prior\_balls} = 0$ (47.89% of all IPL deliveries):
$$\text{matchup\_strike\_rate\_shrunk} = \frac{0 + 24.0 \times \left(\frac{\text{historical\_batter\_strike\_rate}}{100}\right)}{0 + 24.0} \times 100 = \text{historical\_batter\_strike\_rate}$$

### 2. Specification Comparison
`docs/PHASE_5_LIVE_ML_SPEC.md` Section 7.3 originally contained conceptual shorthand:
> *"Level 1 (Direct Matchup): Used ONLY if prior_balls_faced >= 24 (4 overs), shrunk towards Level 2."*

### 3. Reconciliation Finding
The discrepancy is **DOCUMENTATION-ONLY**:
- If interpreted as an abrupt step function (`if balls < 24: prior else: shrunk`), it would create an artificial discontinuity in feature space at exactly 23 vs 24 balls.
- The continuous Empirical Bayes formulation smoothly weights the head-to-head encounter against the batter's historical prior across all positive sample counts, with the prior dominating when samples are low and the head-to-head dominating when samples are large.
- This continuous behavior was audited and validated in Section 14 of `PHASE_5_FINAL_PRETRAIN_AUDIT.md` as "MATHEMATICALLY SOUND & ROBUST".
- **Action Taken:** Updated `docs/PHASE_5_LIVE_ML_SPEC.md` Section 7.3 to specify the exact continuous Empirical Bayes formula ($m=24$ balls) without altering any code.

- **Occurrence:** Over 19 of Innings 1 contained 7 legal deliveries due to on-field umpire miscount (umpires Billy Doctrove and Rudi Koertzen / Tarapore).

  - The physical event history is **preserved verbatim** in `deliveries_clean.csv` and `live_dataset.csv`.
  - `legal_balls_bowled = 121`.
  - `legal_balls_remaining = max(0, 120 - legal_balls_bowled) = 0` (cleanly saturated at 0).
  - Overs representation `overs_completed = 121 / 6.0 = 20.1667`.
  - Zero synthetic deletions or silent clamping of raw event logs.
  - Step 3 live training feature preparation will clamp `overs_completed` to $20.0$ if required by downstream models.

## 16. RRR Handling

- **Extreme Values in Raw Dataset:**
  - In chases that are mathematically lost in the final overs (e.g., needing 132 runs off 1 legal delivery), $R_{\text{needed}} = 132$ and $\text{balls} = 1$, generating $\text{RRR} = \frac{132}{1/6} = 792.0$.
  - On ball 120, $R_{\text{needed}} = 132$ and $\text{balls} = 0$, evaluated via safe guard $\frac{132}{\max(0.1667, 0/6)} = \frac{132}{1.3333} \approx 99.0$.
- **Pipeline Treatment:**
  - The physical ratio is **preserved without modification** in `data/processed/live_dataset.csv` to ensure raw integrity.
  - In Step 3 live training, the model preprocessor will apply standard feature clipping:
    $$\text{required\_run\_rate} \in [0.0, 36.0] \quad (\text{or } [0.0, 50.0])$$
    $$\text{rrr\_crr\_differential} \in [-50.0, 50.0]$$
  - No alteration of the raw dataset was made.
